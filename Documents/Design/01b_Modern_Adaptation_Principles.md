# 01b Modern Adaptation Principles

Based on the evolution of the MMO genre and the rise of Live Service and Survival games, Faldoran Prime must balance "Old School" friction with "Modern" accessibility and systems-driven gameplay.

## 1. Friction with Purpose
The transcript highlights that "Friction... created bonds." However, modern design often tries to eliminate it.
- **Rule:** Do not eliminate friction (e.g., travel time, role dependency, risk) just to make the game "smooth."
- **Goal:** Use friction to create value. If a task is easy, the result is hollow. If a task requires negotiation or effort, the result is a memory.
- **Action:** Maintain social dependency. No instant cues, no universal fast travel. If you want a resource, you must go there or trade for it.

## 2. Designing for Interruption
"The game works around your life."
- **Rule:** A player should be able to contribute meaningfully to the Civilization in 30-60 minute sessions.
- **Goal:** Avoid "attendance-based" progression (mandatory 8-hour raids). Focus on "intention-based" progression.
- **Action:** Implement "Civilization Progress" where small individual actions (crafting a few swords, gathering wood) fuel the larger Node expansion, allowing casual players to feel like vital citizens.

## 3. Systems Over Checklists
"You didn't explore, you followed paths... Leveling turned into tutorial."
- **Rule:** Minimize hand-crafted "!" quests. Maximize emergent systems.
- **Goal:** The world should generate stories, not just content.
- **Action:** Instead of "Kill 10 Rats," use "The Rat Infestation is depleting the Grain Silo, impacting town food supply." The latter creates an economic and survival motive for player action.

## 4. Risk as Narrative (The "High Stakes" Return)
"Risk creates meaning. Without risk, accomplishments feel hollow."
- **Rule:** Death and failure must have consequences that ripple into the world.
- **Goal:** A "close call" should be a story shared in a tavern.
- **Action:** High-risk zones (Wilderness, Irradiated) should have higher loot rewards but significant "Corpse Run" tension. The current death system (Inventory Drop) is the floor; environmental hazards and PvP should add layers of risk that make survival feel earned.

## 5. Identity & Reputation (The "Server Legend")
"Your character name mattered because you couldn't hide behind matchmaking."
- **Rule:** Anonymity is the enemy of community. 
- **Goal:** Players should recognize names and guild tags as they do in a real town.
- **Action:** Resist cross-realm matchmaking. Keep servers as persistent communities. Use the **Titles** system to ensure a player's expertise is visible and respected.

## 6. Living World Persistence
"Logging back in felt like a returning to a place, not booting up a product."
- **Rule:** The world must change while the player is offline.
- **Goal:** Resource scarcity, territory shifts, and seasonal changes should greet the player upon return.
- **Action:** Ensure the **Node System** and **Resource Migration** are visible even in the UI/Map, so players feel the "pulse" of the world even during short sessions.

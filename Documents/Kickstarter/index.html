The MMO You Were Promised — Finally Being Built

Remember when MMOs felt like worlds instead of lobby screens?

When your character's name meant something. When losing a fight had real consequences. When the economy wasn't a theme park gift shop — it was a living, breathing engine driven by real player choices.

That feeling didn't die. The industry just stopped building for it.

Faldoran Prime is an MMO-Survival Hybrid built on a single conviction: the most compelling content in an online world is other people.

Not scripted cutscenes. Not fetch quests. People. The blacksmith who forged your armor. The guild that controls the only portal to the southern continent. The caravan you escorted through bandit territory at 2 AM.

We're not making another game. We're making a world worth living in.

Five Pillars. One Living World.

Every system in Faldoran Prime connects back to a single philosophy: your actions reshape the world for everyone.

⛰️

The Living World

Dynamic Scarcity

Resources migrate. Forests regrow — slowly. Seasons change what's available. Deplete a region and watch it transform from a boom town into a ghost town. The planet is Earth-sized and finite. Iron veins don't respawn. When a mountain is stripped, it's empty — forever.

🤝

Interdependence

No One Does It Alone

No single player can master everything. High-tier crafting demands materials from the deepest caves — so the blacksmith hires the mercenary. Food rots. Gear breaks. Every profession is a lifeline, not a hobby. You need other people. That's not a limitation — it's the entire point.

🏰

Civilization Building

From Campfire to Kingdom

Claim territory. Lease land. Build guild towns with structural physics. Terraform your holdings. Construct portals that link continents — then charge a toll. Mint your own currency. Run a bank. This isn't base-building. It's nation-building.

⚔️

Meaningful Risk

Stakes Create Stories

Death isn't a 10-second respawn timer. It's a durability hit on your gear, a percentage of your inventory scattered for anyone to loot, and Soul Debt that makes your skills rusty until you earn your edge back. Every fight matters. Every escape becomes a story you tell in the tavern.

🌊

The Frontier

Oceans, Caravans & Discovery

Crossing a major ocean is a multi-day real-time voyage. Your ship is your home, your workshop, and your fortress. Caravans roll along guild-maintained highways while you sleep. The unknown is vast. The reward for reaching it is everything.

What Makes This Different

A Classless World

There are no classes. No hard level caps. You are defined by what you do. Swing a sword — your swordsmanship grows. Brew potions — your alchemy deepens. Stop practicing for too long and you'll get rusty — your skills decay, your hands slow — but you never forget. A Rank 100 Chef returning after a year can still cook a masterwork meal — they might just burn a few first.

At creation, you set Affinity Multipliers — Martial, Ranged, Magic, Crafting, Social, Survival — shaping how quickly you develop, but never locking you in.

150+ Individual skills across 12 categories — from Pyromancy to Selective Breeding to Secret Passage Architecture

Crafting That Matters

Forget recipe books. In Faldoran Prime, every item starts with a Base Template — then you infuse it. Iron for damage. Gemstones for crystal efficiency. Rare hides for elemental resistance. Every resource you add increases the item's power — and its Volatility.

Push too far and the item shatters in your hands, consuming every material you invested. High-skill crafters stabilize the process. No two items are ever the same. A master blacksmith's steel sword will have five infusion slots where a novice's has one.

∞ Unique items. No two crafted objects share identical stats, infusions, or histories.

A 100% Player-Driven Economy

There is no Auction House. No global marketplace with instant delivery. Guilds mint their own currencies, backed by physical resources — gold, iron, grain — held in their vaults. Value is determined by trust and regional demand.

Iron in the North. Spices in the South. That gap creates Silk Road economics — trade routes that need protection, caravans that need guards, bandits who see opportunity. When a region's resources are exhausted, local prices skyrocket, guilds liquidate their towns for raw materials, and the whole alliance migrates to fresh territory.

0 NPC vendors with infinite stock. Every sword, every meal, every brick was made by a player.

Combat With Weight

Forget armor "sets." Characters have 20+ skeletal sockets — shoulder, forearm, thigh, shin, chest-core, neck, crown. A "chest piece" might be a leather vest, a steel plate, and a bandolier — three separate items, each breakable individually when struck.

The body adapts to the energy it channels. Warriors build HP and stamina. Mages sacrifice physical resilience for Arcane Attunement — and rely on Mana Crystals as external batteries instead of internal mana pools. Heavy armor dampens crystal resonance. A mage in plate is a mage who can't cast.

20+ Modular gear sockets per character. Granular hit-zone damage. Every piece tells a story of wear.

Questions We Get Asked

Is this just another survival game?

No. Survival mechanics (hunger, gear decay, environmental hazards) exist, but they serve the civilization-building loop. You're not struggling to survive alone in a forest — you're building the supply chain that feeds a guild town of 200 players. The survival pressure is what makes every profession essential and every player valuable.

How is this different from Ashes of Creation?

We share DNA with Ashes in the node/territory space, but diverge sharply in three areas: (1) Our world is procedurally generated at planetary scale with finite, non-respawning resources — not a handcrafted map. (2) We are fully classless with skill decay, not archetype-based. (3) Our economy is 100% player-driven with guild-minted currencies — there is no NPC vendor with infinite stock anywhere in the game.

How big is the team?

We are a small, focused indie team operating under Celtic Trinity Studios. Kickstarter funds will directly expand the team — particularly in environment art, server engineering, and QA. We believe in building in public: our backers see real builds, not marketing renders.

What about griefing? This sounds hardcore.

Hardcore ≠ grief-friendly. Vulnerability Windows mean your base can only be attacked during specific declared times — no more logging in to a destroyed town. Security Tiers (Sanctuary → Settled → Wilderness → Irradiated) let players choose their risk level. Settled zones protect casual players while the Wilderness rewards those who seek danger.

Can I play solo or casually?

Absolutely. The "Designing for Interruption" pillar ensures that 30–60 minute sessions are meaningful. Craft a few swords, tend your farm, refine materials — every small action fuels the larger civilization. You don't need to be a hardcore raider to matter. You just need to contribute.

What platforms will this be on?

PC first. Built in Unreal Engine 5. Console versions are a post-launch goal, not a launch promise. We won't compromise the server architecture or UI for cross-platform parity during development.

What's the business model after launch?

We are committed to no pay-to-win. The current plan is a modest monthly subscription with zero gameplay-affecting microtransactions. Cosmetic-only store items may be offered, but nothing that provides a statistical advantage. The economy must remain player-driven to function — injecting items through a cash shop would destroy the game's core loop.

What happens if you don't hit the funding goal?

Development continues regardless — we're already building. Kickstarter funding accelerates the timeline and expands the feature set available at Early Access launch. The stretch goals represent features that are designed — funding determines whether they ship at launch or post-launch.
